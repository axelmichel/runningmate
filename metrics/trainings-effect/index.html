    Trainings Effect

    The Effect tab of the detail view rates what an activity did for your fitness in the way Garmin, Suunto and Polar watches do with the method of Firstbeat: from EPOC, the excess post-exercise oxygen consumption, the extra oxygen your body needs after exercise to return to rest. The harder and longer you train, the higher EPOC climbs; easy stretches let it fall again.

    From the heart rate of the activity RunningMate works out

    • the peak EPOC in ml of oxygen per kg of body weight,
    • the aerobic training effect from 0.0 to 5.0, from the peak EPOC and your fitness level,
    • the anaerobic training effect from 0.0 to 5.0, from the time close to your maximum heart rate,
    • the recovery time in hours.

    Firstbeat's model is proprietary. Its published form (the white paper EPOC Based Training Effect Assessment) is followed here, but the formulas and constants below are RunningMate's own approximation: the values come close to what a watch shows, but they won't be the same.

    Beside it, the Effect tab shows the effect score from 1 to 12, RunningMate's own rating carried over from the first version of the app. It compares the activity with your training of the last 90 days instead.

    Data used

    • The heart rate of every trackpoint of the archived activity file. Each point's heart rate counts for the time since the previous point. Without a recorded track, the splits are used instead: each split's average heart rate for its duration. Without any heart rate there is no training effect.
    • Your maximum and resting heart rate from the user settings. When one is missing, a fallback is used (see Fallbacks); the Effect tab says which.
    • Your VO2max for the fitness level: estimated from your runs of the 90 days up to the activity, as on the Health page, or the value from your profile when there are not enough runs.

    Intensity

    The intensity is the share of the heart-rate reserve, the range between resting and maximum heart rate (as in the Karvonen zones):

    kept between 0 and 1. The share of the heart-rate reserve is close to the share of the oxygen uptake reserve, which is why Firstbeat and others use it to measure the intensity from the heart rate.

    EPOC

    EPOC is accumulated step by step: . At a constant intensity it moves exponentially towards a steady state that depends on the intensity: upwards when the intensity is higher than what the current EPOC corresponds to, downwards (recovery) when it is lower.

    EPOC starts at 0. The peak EPOC is the highest value during the activity. Because the time constant grows with the intensity, EPOC keeps climbing during long hard efforts, while easy running levels off early: an hour at 65 % of the reserve ends at about 22 ml/kg, 45 minutes at 82 % at about 81 ml/kg.

    Pauses. As for the activity data, a gap of more than 10 seconds between two trackpoints is a pause. A pause, and any stretch without heart rate, counts as rest (): EPOC only decays during it, so a pause never adds EPOC, whatever the heart rate after it.

    The scale

    Both effects use the usual wording:

    Effect Meaning
    0.0–0.9 No effect
    1.0–1.9 Minor
    2.0–2.9 Maintaining
    3.0–3.9 Improving
    4.0–4.9 Highly improving
    5.0 Overreaching

    A maximum heart rate set too low makes every activity look harder, a resting heart rate set too high as well. The Effect tab lists the heart rates and the VO2max it used and where they come from.

    Things to keep in mind

    • The training effect is based on heart rate only. A heart rate that runs high from heat, caffeine, illness or a slipping chest strap raises it; one that lags behind short sprints lowers the anaerobic effect.
    • The aerobic effect depends on your fitness level, so the same run rates lower as your VO2max rises.
    • Your heart rate zones aren't used, except for the zone 5 bound as a fallback for the maximum heart rate; they are only used for the time in zones.
    • The fitness, fatigue and form chart on the same tab is described under Fitness and form.

    The effect score (1–12)

    The effect score is a simpler rating that doesn't need your maximum or resting heart rate. It compares how hard the activity was with how hard your activities of the same sport were in the 90 days before it, using the averages of the splits.

    Load. For runs and walks the load of a split is its average heart rate times its pace (min/km) over its speed (m/s) plus one; for rides the power (W) takes the place of the pace:

    Baseline. The mean load of all splits of the same sport in the 90 days before the activity, at least 10 (10 without any). Splits without heart rate, pace or speed (and, for rides, power) are left out, so a run recorded without a strap doesn't pull the baseline down.

    Score. The activity's load from the averages of all its splits is weighted by how high its heart rate was and compared with the baseline :

    An activity missing any of the averages scores 1. The suggested rest is hours, from about 8 hours for 1 to 5 days for 12.

    Score Meaning
    1–2 Recovery
    3–5 Base endurance
    6–7 Tempo
    8–9 Threshold
    10–11 VO2max
    12 Anaerobic

    Because the score is relative to your own recent training, the same run scores lower after a block of hard weeks than after a quiet month. Unlike the training effect, it doesn't know your heart-rate reserve or fitness, and as the load uses pace, a slow run at a high heart rate scores high.
