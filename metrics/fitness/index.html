    Fitness and form

    The Health page and the Effect tab of the detail view show how your training adds up over weeks: fitness, fatigue and form from a training-load model, and a cardio fitness estimate (VO2max) from your runs.

    Fitness, fatigue and form

    RunningMate uses a training-load model in the style of Banister's fitness-fatigue model, as TrainingPeaks and intervals.icu show it (CTL, ATL and TSB):

    • Fitness is a slow average of your training load, over about 42 training days.
    • Fatigue is a fast average, over about 7 training days.
    • Form is fitness minus fatigue. After hard weeks fatigue is above fitness and form is negative; after easy days it turns positive.

    Load of an activity

    Every split with a heart rate and a pace has a load

    with the average heart rate (bpm), the pace (min/km) and the speed (m/s) of the split, also for rides. (The training effect uses its own model, based on EPOC.) An activity's load is the mean of its splits' loads, weighted by the activity type:

    Activity Weight
    Run 1.0
    Ride 0.8
    Walk 0.5

    The loads of all activities on the same day (by UTC date) are added up to the day's load. Splits without heart rate have no load, and an activity recorded without heart rate isn't a training day at all, so it doesn't pull the curve down.

    Unlike TrainingPeaks, the averages only move on days with training: a rest day doesn't lower fitness or fatigue, the next activity does. Form includes the day's own training.

    The curve is always computed from your first activity on, also when the chart only shows a part of it, so a day has the same values in every period you look at.

    On the Effect tab

    The detail view shows fitness, fatigue and form for the 90 days up to the activity, as raw values.

    On the Health page

    The load numbers have no unit anyone knows, so the Health page shows them as shares:

    • Fitness is your fitness on the last training day of the period as a share of the highest fitness you ever reached up to the end of the period, your personal best. Below it is the change over the period, from the first to the last training day in it: %.
    • Form is form as a share of fitness, %, rated in Joe Friel's zones as intervals.icu shows them:
    Form Zone Meaning
    below −30 % High risk Fatigue is far above your fitness; too much of this risks overtraining.
    −30 to −10 % Optimal A productive training load: your fitness is building.
    −10 to +5 % Grey zone Maintaining: not much building, not much recovering.
    +5 to +20 % Fresh Rested and fit: a good time for a race or a test.
    above +20 % Transition Very rested: without training your fitness starts to drop.

    A value on a boundary belongs to the zone above it (−10 % is the grey zone). In the example above, form is % of fitness: Optimal.

    Cardio fitness (VO2max)

    VO2max is the most oxygen your body can use, in ml per kg of body weight per minute. RunningMate has no lab or watch measurement, so it estimates it from your runs with Jack Daniels' and Jimmy Gilbert's VDOT formula: the oxygen cost of the pace you ran, divided by the share of VO2max you can hold for that long.

    For a run of metres in minutes of moving time (duration minus pauses), with the velocity in metres per minute:

    • Only runs of at least 3 km in the chosen period count.
    • Results below 15 or above 85 are treated as recording errors and dropped.
    • At least 3 runs are needed. Training runs aren't all-out races, so the estimate is the 90th percentile of the runs' values: it reflects your harder runs without trusting a single outlier (for example a run with a wrongly recorded pause).
    • With fewer runs, the VO2max from your user settings is shown instead, if you entered one.

    Example: 5 km in 25:00 gives m/min, a cost of about 36.0 and a share of about 0.94, so a VO2max of about 38.3.

    Rating

    With your birthday and sex in the user settings, the estimate is compared with people of the same sex and age group (20–29, 30–39 … 70–79; younger or older ages use the nearest group), using the Cooper Institute norms that Garmin uses. The age is your age at the end of the period. The levels start at these values (ml/kg/min):

    Age Men: Fair Good Excellent Superior Women: Fair Good Excellent Superior
    20–29 41.7 45.4 51.1 55.4 36.1 39.5 43.9 49.6
    30–39 40.5 44.0 48.3 54.0 34.4 37.8 42.4 47.4
    40–49 38.5 42.4 46.4 52.5 33.0 36.3 39.7 45.3
    50–59 35.6 39.2 43.4 48.9 30.1 33.0 36.7 41.1
    60–69 32.3 35.5 39.5 45.7 27.5 30.0 33.0 37.8
    70–79 29.4 32.3 36.7 42.1 25.9 28.1 30.9 36.7

    Below the Fair value the level is Poor. In the example, 38.3 is Good for a woman of 45 and Fair for a man of 55.
